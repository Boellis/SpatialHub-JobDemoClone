# Mars to Table — Submission Walkthrough Video: Script + Storyboard

**Deliverable 5 of 6** · NASA Deep Space Food Challenge: *Mars to Table*
Format: narrated video ≤ 10:00 · 15-crew · 500-sol surface mission · 3,035 kcal/crew/day · ~34% Earth / ~66% in-situ calories

> This is the production script and shot-by-shot storyboard for the submission walkthrough video. It opens on a hook, **leads with the live monitoring & control system (our differentiator)**, then walks the Concept of Operations (four mission points), the Design Layout (Zones A–E), ties everything back to the 14-Sol Meal Plan, and closes on terrestrial potential.
> All quantitative values are representative design estimates pending human verification (Human-in-the-Loop mandate).

---

## Production summary

- **Total runtime:** 10:00 (cold-open 0:00–0:30 through closeout 9:30–10:00).
- **Narration budget:** ~1,400 words at ~150 wpm. Each segment lists its visual, verbatim voiceover, and burned-in caption text.
- **Voice:** single calm narrator, conversational but precise. Music bed low, ducked under VO.
- **Sequencing logic:** hook → differentiator → ConOps → layout → meal-plan tie-back → terrestrial closeout, so the strongest scoring asset lands first while attention is highest.

## Shot table

### Segment 1 — Cold open / hook · `0:00–0:30`

- **VISUAL:** Slow push-in on the Mars habitat exterior render at dawn, dust settling. Cut to a single fresh strawberry being plated under LED grow-light. Title card resolves: **"Mars to Table — a near-closed-loop surface food system."** Lower-third: *15 crew · 500 sols.*
- **NARRATION:** "Fifteen people. Five hundred sols on the Martian surface. Three thousand thirty-five calories each, every single day — and no resupply truck coming. The question isn't whether we can pack enough food. It's whether we can *grow* it, *recycle* it, and *trust* it. This is Mars to Table: a food system that feeds a crew a full fourteen-sol menu while recycling its own water and waste — and watches itself in real time."
- **CAPTION:** "15 crew · 500 sols · 3,035 kcal/day · no resupply. A food system that grows, recycles, and monitors itself."

---

### Segment 2 — The differentiator: live monitoring & control · `0:30–2:30`

- **VISUAL:** Screen-capture of the live SSE web dashboard. Real-time gauges for O₂, CO₂, water, biomass, and power tick upward. Cut to the habitat dashboard showing the state pill move **standby → live → pause → live**. Overlay the autonomous control-loop view adjusting production rates; resource-balance lines settle toward zero net. Pan the durable decision log — timestamped control actions scrolling. Lower-third throughout: *Live Monitoring & Control.*
- **NARRATION:** "Here's what sets this team apart, so we're leading with it. This is our live monitoring and control system — and it is built, not just drawn. Every store in the habitat — oxygen, carbon dioxide, water, biomass, power — streams to this dashboard in real time. Behind it runs an autonomous control loop: it reads each buffer and adjusts production rates to hold every resource inside a safe band, with no one in the loop. We ran it against NASA's BioSim life-support simulator under a malfunctions profile, and it drove the habitat to a self-sustaining, closed-loop equilibrium — every resource balance trending to roughly zero net — across a clean five-hundred-sol run. Watch the buffers settle. Nothing is drifting toward a cliff. And every action the controller takes is written to a durable decision log — this scrolling record. That log is our auditability and our predictive maintenance: per-store burn-down flags a shortfall before it becomes a failure. This habitat view mirrors the live test — standby, live, pause, resume, stop — so an operator always knows exactly what the system is doing. That's the difference between a food system on paper and one you can actually run."
- **CAPTION:** "Live SSE dashboard + autonomous control loop. Held a closed-loop equilibrium across a clean 500-sol BioSim run. Every control action logged — auditable, predictive."

---

### Segment 3 — Concept of Operations: the four mission points · `2:30–4:30`

- **VISUAL:** Block diagram of the food system as a node inside ECLSS — light/power, CO₂, recycled water in; meals, recovered water, compost/biogas out. Then a four-panel timeline animates through the mission points: Startup, Nominal, Contingency, Shutdown. Annotate water and nutrient/waste loops as the primary couplings; air exchange tagged "secondary." Lower-third: *Concept of Operations.*
- **NARRATION:** "How does it actually operate over five hundred sols? Four phases. *Startup:* on arrival, the crew energizes the racks, charges the water loop from cached and in-situ water, inoculates the fermenters and aquaponics, and stagger-seeds crops by maturity — greens in about three weeks, grains and tubers in ten to fourteen. Earth provisions bridge the gap, capped at fifty percent of calories, until in-situ output ramps. *Nominal operations:* automated light and climate cycles, harvest-to-order greens and fish, weekly batch milling and fermentation — keeping crew time under nine hours a sol. *Contingency:* a power dip sheds non-essential lighting first while batteries hold aeration, fermenters, and the cold chain; a water restriction tightens recycling and prioritizes the drinking and aquaponic loops; EVA and metabolic swings are absorbed by portion scaling and a two-hundred-calorie-per-EVA-hour supplement. *Shutdown:* a graceful safing that protects living systems longest and checkpoints the loop state, so the next crew restarts warm, not cold. The food system augments life support mainly through its water and nutrient loops; crop air exchange is a real but secondary effect — mechanical oxygen and CO₂ removal stay primary."
- **CAPTION:** "ConOps — Startup · Nominal · Contingency · Shutdown. Couples to ECLSS via water + nutrient loops (air exchange secondary). Earth bridge capped at 50%."

### Segment 4 — Design Layout: Zones A–E flythrough · `4:30–6:45`

- **VISUAL:** Blueprint of the habitat pulls back, then a camera flies zone to zone. Each zone gets a labeled stop with its production lines highlighted. **A** — vertical 3-tier crop bays glowing under grow-light. **B** — salad and fruiting racks, strawberries and microgreens. **C** — bioreactors: aquaponic tilapia tank, fungi blocks, spirulina photobioreactor, fermenters bubbling. **D** — processing and galley: mill, press, ferment, cook-chill, communal table. **E** — resource and waste: water recycling, nutrient mixing, composter/digester, dry storage. Lower-third per stop: *Zone A … Zone E.*
- **NARRATION:** "Now the layout — five zones, each a job. *Zone A, the Vertical Crop Bays:* three-tier hydroponic racks growing the calorie staples — dwarf wheat, white and sweet potato, rice, soybean, beans, peanut. Highest calories per square meter, soil-less, spread across isolatable bays for redundancy. *Zone B, Fresh and Salad Racks:* LED-lit greens, fruiting vegetables, strawberries, microgreens — fast-turnover, the morale crops. *Zone C, Bioreactors:* an aquaponic tilapia loop that turns fish effluent into fertilizer, a fungi bioreactor that grows mushrooms on crop residue, a spirulina photobioreactor, and the fermenters for tempeh, miso, soy sauce, kimchi, and yeast protein. Five protein pathways, so no single failure starves the crew. *Zone D, Processing and Galley:* the mill, the press, the fermenters, cook-chill, and the communal dining table where the crew actually eats together. *Zone E, Resource and Waste:* water recycling, nutrient mixing, the composter and digester, and dry storage — the loop-closer. Roughly three hundred square meters of multi-tier grow area, water recycled above ninety percent, powered by a nuclear primary with battery backup for the life-critical lines."
- **CAPTION:** "5 zones — A Crop Bays · B Salad Racks · C Bioreactors · D Processing & Galley · E Resource & Waste. ~300 m² grow area · >90% water recycled · nuclear + battery backup."

### Segment 5 — Tie to the 14-Sol Meal Plan · `6:45–8:30`

- **VISUAL:** The 14-sol menu grid fades up. Camera lands on one sol — say Sol 1, Mediterranean. As each dish is named, animated lines trace from the dish to its production line in the zone map, to a recycling loop, and to a monitored buffer on the dashboard. Then a fast montage of the social anchors (Sol 7, Sol 14 multi-course feasts). Calorie-source donut resolves: 66% in-situ / 34% Earth. Lower-third: *14-Sol Meal Plan.*
- **NARRATION:** "And this is the payoff — because every dish on the fourteen-sol menu traces to a production line, a recycling loop, and a monitored buffer on this system. Take Sol One: herb-baked tilapia comes from the Zone C aquaponic loop, whose effluent fertilizes the greens; the roasted potatoes and sourdough come from the Zone A staples; the strawberries are a Zone B harvest. The water that grew all of it cycles back through Zone E. Fourteen distinct daily menus, no main dish repeated, a cuisine theme that rotates so novelty holds across thirty-five cycles. Sol Seven and Sol Fourteen are deliberate communal feasts — a multi-course countermeasure to food monotony, because morale is a mission risk. Across the plan, calories land at about sixty-six percent grown on Mars and thirty-four percent from Earth — sixteen points under the fifty-percent cap, so Earth food stays an enhancement, never a necessity. Batch cooking and automation keep the meal specialist near six to seven hours a sol, well under the nine-hour budget."
- **CAPTION:** "Every dish → a production line, a recycling loop, a monitored buffer. 14 menus, no repeats. ~66% in-situ / ~34% Earth (16 pts under cap). ~6–7 h/sol crew time."

### Segment 6 — Safety, resilience & human factors · `8:30–9:30`

- **VISUAL:** Split-screen: HACCP critical-control-points overlay on the processing line (cook-chill temperature logs, ferment pH); redundancy diagram showing staples across ≥2 bays and the multiple protein lines; a power-loss load-shed animation. Then crew at the communal table. Lower-third: *Safety · Resilience · Human Factors.*
- **NARRATION:** "Trust comes from safety and resilience. Food safety is HACCP-style: defined control points from production through cooking to storage, with temperature logging and fermentation pH control. Resilience is engineered in — staples duplicated across isolatable bays, five independent protein pathways, prioritized load-shed on power loss, and the Earth bridge as the ultimate backstop. And it's built for a crew without deep farming expertise: guided dashboard workflows, standardized procedures, and simple assembly cooking. The system does the watching so the crew can do the living."
- **CAPTION:** "HACCP control points · redundant bays · 5 protein pathways · prioritized load-shed. Built for non-expert operators — guided workflows, standardized procedures."

Fifteen crew, five hundred sols, a full menu — grown, recycled, and watched in real time. That's Mars to Table."
- **CAPTION:** "Modular CEA for Earth — polar, disaster, remote food security. Mars to Table: grown, recycled, monitored — start to finish."

## Captions / accessibility

- **Burned-in subtitles** on every segment (open captions), styled for legibility: high-contrast white text, dark semi-opaque backing bar, sans-serif, ≤ 2 lines on screen at a time, timed to the VO.
- A separate `.srt` sidecar is exported for platforms that support closed captions, matching the burned-in text verbatim.
- **Consistent lower-thirds** name the current section (*Live Monitoring & Control*, *Concept of Operations*, *Zone A…E*, *14-Sol Meal Plan*, *Safety · Resilience*, etc.) in the same position and style throughout, so a judge can orient at any timestamp.
- No information is conveyed by color alone; every gauge/state change is also labeled in text or VO.

## Graphics kit

- **Palette:** deep space charcoal `#0E1116` base; Mars rust accent `#C1502E`; in-situ growth green `#3FA66A`; live-data cyan `#39C0D6` reserved for dashboard/telemetry; off-white `#F2EFE9` for text. In-situ vs Earth always green vs rust.
- **Typography:** one geometric sans for headers/lower-thirds (e.g., Inter/Space Grotesk), one mono for telemetry readouts and the decision-log scroll — consistent across all titles, captions, and overlays.
- **Motion:** consistent ease-in-out transitions; trace-lines (dish → production line → loop → buffer) use the same animated stroke style throughout; dashboard screen-captures kept at native UI scale for authenticity.
- **Consistency rule:** the same zone colors, icons, and lower-third treatment appear in the ConOps block diagram, the layout flythrough, and the meal-plan trace — one visual language end to end.
